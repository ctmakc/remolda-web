# Google Business Profile Optimization for Canadian Small Businesses

For a Canadian small business — whether you run a restaurant in Montreal, a hair salon in Ottawa, or a plumbing service in Calgary — your Google Business Profile (formerly Google My Business) is your most important digital storefront. When a potential customer searches "Italian restaurant Plateau-Mont-Royal" or "emergency plumber Ottawa", your Google Business Profile determines whether you appear in the coveted "Local Pack" — the top three results that dominate mobile search results pages and capture the vast majority of local clicks.

Yet most Canadian small businesses significantly underutilise their Google Business Profile. An incomplete listing, mediocre photos, unanswered reviews, and zero posts: this is the typical picture we see at Remolda when auditing client profiles. This guide shows you how to transform your listing into a powerful local customer generation tool.

## Why Google Business Profile is Critical for Canadian Businesses

### The Impact on Local Visibility

In 2025, 97% of Canadians use the internet to search for local businesses. Among them, 86% use Google Maps or Google Search to find a business address. A well-optimised Google Business Profile:
- Appears in the Local Pack (the top three map results)
- Displays in Google Maps
- Generates direct phone calls (the "Call" button on mobile)
- Drives traffic to your website
- Shows your hours and essential information without requiring a click

### The Competitive Advantage

In Canada, many small businesses have created a Google Business Profile but never optimised it. A well-maintained, fully optimised listing stands out immediately against competitors with incomplete profiles. This is one of the few areas where small businesses can gain a genuine competitive advantage without a significant advertising budget — simply by doing the basics well.

## Setup and Verification: The Foundations

### Creating or Claiming Your Listing

If your business has existed for several years, Google may have automatically created a listing for you. Check first by searching your business name on Google Maps. If the listing exists but is unclaimed, you can recover it via the "Claim this business" button.

To create a new listing: go to business.google.com, sign in with your Google account, and follow the creation process. Provide complete, accurate information from the start — it's easier than fixing incomplete information later.

### Verification: A Required Step

Google requires you to verify your business before you can edit your public listing. Available methods vary by business:

**Postcard verification**: Google sends a postcard to your business address with a 5-digit code. Timeframe: 5–14 days. This is the most common method for new listings.

**Phone or text verification**: available for some businesses with a verified phone number.

**Instant verification**: sometimes available if your business is already verified in Google Search Console with the same Google account.

**Practical tip**: don't change your listing address during the verification process, as this cancels the postcard in transit.

## Categories and Attributes: The Foundation of Local SEO

### Choosing the Right Primary Category

Your primary category is the most important ranking factor in your listing. Google uses it to determine which searches trigger your profile's appearance.

**Golden rule**: choose the category that most precisely matches your primary activity, not the most generic one that seems to fit. A business categorised as "Vietnamese Restaurant" will easily outrank a competitor categorised as simply "Restaurant" for relevant searches.

You can add up to 9 secondary categories. Use them to cover other aspects of your business. For example, a hair salon: primary category "Hair Salon", secondary categories "Hair Colouring Service", "Wedding Hair Salon", "Hair Extensions".

### Attributes: The Details That Make the Difference

Attributes are additional characteristics describing your business that influence filtered searches. The most important for Canadian businesses:

- **Accessibility**: wheelchair accessible entrance, accessible parking
- **Payment**: cards accepted, contactless payment, Apple Pay/Google Pay
- **Services**: delivery available, online ordering, reservation required
- **Atmosphere**: family-friendly, pet-friendly (patio)
- **Languages**: multilingual staff (highly valuable for bilingual markets)

**Canadian specificity**: the "Bilingual service (French/English)" language attribute is particularly valued in regions with high bilingual populations such as Ottawa, Montreal, and New Brunswick. Explicitly indicating bilingual service capability in your attributes and description can differentiate you from competitors who have overlooked this.

## Review Management: The Key to Local Reputation

### Why Google Reviews are Critical

Google reviews directly influence both your local ranking AND potential customers' decisions. According to BrightLocal, 87% of consumers read online reviews for local businesses, and 79% trust online reviews as much as personal recommendations.

An average score of 4.3/5 with 50 reviews will generally outperform a competitor with 4.8/5 but only 8 reviews — in terms of overall trustworthiness and ranking impact. Volume and recency both matter.

### Requesting Reviews: Methods That Work

**The direct approach (most effective)**: ask in person, immediately after delivering an exceptional service. "Would you be willing to leave us a Google review? It really helps us." Most satisfied customers will agree — but they need to be asked. The ask works best in the moment of positive feeling, not days or weeks later.

**The direct link**: create a short link to your Google review page (available in your Google Business dashboard under "Get more reviews"). Embed this link in your follow-up emails, confirmation texts, and email signature.

**The QR code**: print a QR code leading directly to your review page and place it on receipts, business cards, or at your reception desk.

**What you should NEVER do**: buy fake reviews, ask friends and family to leave reviews (Google can detect and remove them), offer discounts in exchange for reviews (violation of Google's guidelines that can result in listing suspension).

### Responding to Reviews: An Act of SEO and Customer Service

Responding to all your reviews — positive AND negative — has a dual impact:
1. **SEO**: Google confirms that responding to reviews improves local ranking
2. **Brand image**: potential customers read your responses as carefully as the reviews themselves

**Responding to positive reviews**: thank sincerely, personalise (mention a specific detail from the review), and invite them back. Example: "Thank you so much, Sarah, for this lovely feedback! We're thrilled that your experience with our team was positive. We hope to see you again soon at [Business Name]!"

**Responding to negative reviews**: remain professional and empathetic, don't defend yourself publicly, offer to resolve the issue offline. Example: "We're sorry to hear your experience didn't meet your expectations. Could you please contact us directly at [phone number] so we can work together to find a solution?"

## Google Posts: Keeping Your Listing Active

Google Posts are short-duration publications (7 days for standard posts) that appear directly on your Google Business Profile. They signal to Google that your listing is active and provide additional surface area to communicate with potential customers.

### Types of Posts and Their Uses

**"What's New" post**: general news about your business, special hours, new product lines. Ideal for regular communication and keeping your listing fresh.

**"Offer" post**: time-limited promotions with start and end dates. Displays with an "Offer" badge in local results — higher visibility.

**"Event" post**: themed evenings, workshops, product launches. Displays with event dates and time.

**"Product" post**: highlight a specific product or service with a photo and order/booking link.

### Publication Strategy for Maximum Impact

**Recommended frequency**: 1–2 posts per week. Consistency matters more than volume.

**Optimal format**:
- High-quality photo (minimum 1,200 × 900 px)
- Concise text: 150–300 words
- Clear CTA: "Book now", "Claim offer", "Learn more"

**Canadian seasonal calendar**: align your posts with Canadian seasons and events — winter services (snowplowing, heating maintenance) in fall, patio opening in spring, back-to-school in August–September, Christmas, Valentine's Day, Canada Day (July 1st), and for Quebec-targeted businesses, Fête nationale du Québec (June 24th).

## Q&A Section: Control the Conversation

The Questions and Answers section of your Google listing is often ignored — this is a mistake. Anyone can ask questions, and anyone can answer them (including misinformed people or malicious competitors).

**Proactive strategy**: ask the most common questions yourself and answer them. This controls the narrative and reduces repetitive phone calls.

Examples of proactive Q&As:
- "Do you accept credit card payments?" → "Yes, we accept Visa, Mastercard, American Express, and contactless payment."
- "Is there free parking?" → "Yes, we have free customer parking at the rear of the building."
- "Do you offer home service?" → [Appropriate answer]

**Monitoring**: check regularly for new questions and respond within 24 hours.

## Photo Optimisation

Photos are a conversion factor that is often underestimated. According to Google, listings with photos receive 42% more direction requests and 35% more clicks to their website.

### Essential Photos

- **Cover photo**: exterior view of your establishment (helps customers recognise you)
- **Logo**: your logo in high resolution
- **Interior photos**: 5–10 photos showing the atmosphere and space
- **Product/service photos**: your best products, dishes, or completed work
- **Team photos**: your team at work (humanises the business)

### Quality and Frequency

Post professional or very high-quality photos. A recent smartphone is sufficient if the lighting is good. Regularly add new photos — Google values active listings with recent content.

## GBP for Multi-Location Businesses

For franchises or businesses with multiple locations across Canada, Google Business Profile offers centralised management tools.

**Location Groups**: group all your listings into a group to manage them from a single dashboard.

**Bulk upload**: for 10+ locations, use an Excel/CSV file to update information in bulk.

**Consistency check**: ensure that the name, address, and phone number (NAP — Name, Address, Phone) are identical across all listings and on your website. Inconsistencies harm ranking. This is particularly important for franchise models where individual franchisees may have made their own updates.

## Canada-Specific Tips: Bilingual Listings

For businesses in Quebec, Ontario, or other bilingual regions, bilingual management of your Google Business Profile can give you a significant competitive advantage.

**Business name**: use your official business name. If you have different names in English and French, you can potentially create separate listings — but only if you have distinct addresses or phone numbers for each.

**Description**: write your description in French if your primary market is francophone, in English if primarily anglophone. For businesses targeting both markets (especially in Ottawa), consider a combined description or clearly indicate bilingual service: "Service available in English and French / Service offert en anglais et en français."

**Bilingual posts**: Ottawa and Montreal businesses frequently publish Google Posts in both English and French, either in the same post or alternating. This signals your bilingual service capability to both Google and prospective customers.

**Review responses**: respond in the language of the review. A customer who leaves a French-language review will appreciate a French response; same for English. This simple practice demonstrates cultural awareness and genuine customer care.

## Measuring Your Performance

In your Google Business dashboard, regularly track:
- **Profile views**: how many times did your listing appear in searches?
- **Direction requests**: how many people requested directions to you?
- **Phone calls**: calls generated directly from your listing
- **Website clicks**: traffic sent to your website
- **Direct vs. discovery searches**: direct searches use your name; discovery searches find your listing via a category or keyword (the latter indicate your broader SEO effectiveness)

These metrics allow you to identify what's working and adjust your strategy accordingly. A sudden drop in impressions after changing your primary category, for example, is a clear signal to reconsider the change.
