# Social Media Strategy for Canadian Restaurants and Food Businesses

In the Canadian food service industry, social media is no longer a bonus — it has become a core business requirement. A restaurant opening in Ottawa without an Instagram presence is like an establishment without a storefront window on a busy shopping street: you may technically exist, but nobody sees you. For caterers, bakeries, cafés, food trucks, and restaurants from Quebec to British Columbia, a consistent social media strategy generates reservations, attracts new customers, and keeps existing ones coming back.

But "being on social media" and "having an effective social media strategy" are two completely different things. Sporadic posting, no visual identity, ignored comments, and content that talks only about promotions — that approach wastes time and delivers nothing. This guide shows you how to build a digital presence that drives real customers through your door.

## Choosing the Right Platforms: Instagram, Facebook, TikTok

### Instagram: The Indispensable Visual Showcase

Instagram remains the primary platform for Canadian restaurants and food businesses. Its visual nature makes it the ideal format for showcasing dish aesthetics, your dining room atmosphere, and your team's craftsmanship.

**Formats to master**:

**Feed posts (carousel and photos)**: your "permanent portfolio". Feed posts remain visible and build your visual brand identity. Ideal for signature dish photography, atmosphere shots, and important announcements.

**Instagram Stories**: ephemeral content (24h) perfect for: special hours, "right now" in the kitchen, flash promotions, polls ("Which new dessert do you prefer?"), and team behind-the-scenes. Stories generate conversational engagement and are seen by your most loyal followers.

**Reels (up to 90 seconds)**: Instagram's algorithm has massively favoured Reels over static posts since 2022. For restaurants: sped-up dish preparation, patio tour, the chef explains a signature dish, "what happens before opening". Reels receive on average 3–5× more organic reach than photo posts.

**Practical advice**: aim for 3 posts per week (1 Reel, 1 carousel, 1 simple photo) + 5–7 Stories per week. Consistency beats perfection — a good photo posted regularly outperforms occasional masterpieces.

### Facebook: The Local Community Hub

Facebook has lost ground with under-35s, but remains dominant for 35–65 year olds — an important demographic for sit-down restaurants and family establishments. Facebook excels at:

- **Events**: Sunday brunches, themed evenings, trivia nights, seasonal parties — Facebook event creation remains very effective for local community engagement
- **Local groups**: join and participate in your neighbourhood or city Facebook groups ("Ottawa Foodies", "Montreal Restaurants", "Toronto Food") — adding value without spamming
- **Local advertising**: Facebook Ads remain less expensive than Google Ads for local targeting with a precise audience

**Don't blindly duplicate**: don't automatically copy all your Instagram content to Facebook. The audience is different, the formats are different. On Facebook, longer captions and "community" posts work better than on Instagram.

### TikTok: The Growth Lever for Under-35s

TikTok has become essential for restaurants targeting Millennials and Gen Z in Canada. TikTok's algorithm is unique: it can propel an account with 0 followers to tens of thousands of views if the content is engaging — impossible on Instagram or Facebook for a new account.

**What works for restaurants on TikTok**:
- Sped-up dish preparation ("ASMR food prep")
- "Day in the life" of a chef or server
- Responding to comments with a video ("You asked how we make our croissants...")
- Food-adjacent trends and challenges
- Honest, authentic behind-the-scenes content (TikTok rewards authenticity over perfection)

**Reality check**: TikTok requires volume (2–5 videos per week to see results), and the format is more casual and spontaneous than Instagram. If you have the creative capacity, it's the channel with the strongest organic growth potential in 2025. If not, prioritise Instagram + Facebook first.

## Content Calendar for Restaurants

Content planning is the difference between a consistent organic presence and panicked Monday morning posts. Here is a practical framework.

### Content Pillars: The 5 Categories

**1. Products and Dishes (40% of content)**: photos and videos of your creations. The visual "heroes" of your menu, daily specials, new seasonal creations. This is your core visual identity.

**2. Behind the Scenes (20%)**: in the kitchen, the chef in action, morning bread preparation, the team before opening. This content humanises your brand and creates emotional connection.

**3. Team and Story (15%)**: employee introductions, anniversaries, the history of the establishment, values. "Meet Marie, our baker for the past 8 years." People connect with people before they connect with brands.

**4. Community Content and UGC (15%)**: sharing customer photos, mentions in articles or media, local collaborations. Third-party endorsement is more credible than self-promotion.

**5. Promotions and Calls to Action (10%)**: events, special offers, reservation openings, new menu launches. Keep this category below 10–15% of total content — too many promotional posts reduce engagement.

### Practical Weekly Framework

| Day | Instagram | Facebook | TikTok (if applicable) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Monday | Reel (behind-the-scenes start of week) | Week's event | Spontaneous video |
| Wednesday | Photo post (signature dish) | Share a local article | — |
| Friday | Carousel (weekend special) | Weekend invitation post | TikTok Reel |
| Sunday | Story (brunch/daily special) | — | — |

**Batch shooting**: plan a 2-hour photo session every week or two weeks. Photograph 10–15 dishes, capture some behind-the-scenes stories, and you have two weeks of content ready to schedule.

## UGC Strategy: Your Customers, Your Best Ambassadors

User Generated Content (UGC) — photos and videos taken by your customers — is the most authentic and least expensive marketing tool at your disposal. According to Nielsen, 92% of consumers trust user-generated content more than traditional advertising.

### Encouraging UGC

**Create "photographable moments"**: a colourful wall, a spectacular dish presentation, a unique view from your patio. Restaurants that think about their "Instagram moments" naturally generate more UGC. Even something as simple as a branded paper bag or distinctive table setting can become a UGC trigger.

**Ask explicitly**: train your team to suggest that satisfied customers share their experience on Instagram and tag your establishment. Place your @instagram handle on menus and table cards.

**Dedicated hashtag**: create a hashtag specific to your restaurant (#CaféDuPlateau, #RestoBerthier) and encourage its use. Display it prominently in your space.

**Mini-contests**: "Share your photo and tag us to win a complimentary dessert on your next visit". Simple, effective, and costs only a few dollars per new content creator.

### Resharing UGC

Before resharing a customer photo, always ask permission (a comment "Can we share your photo?" works in most cases). Once agreed:
- Reshare in Stories (the most natural format for UGC)
- Always credit the original photographer with their @ mention
- Add an authentic caption ("Thanks to @username for this beautiful capture!")

Never reshare without permission — it violates Instagram's terms and can damage the customer relationship.

## Micro-Influencer Partnerships

Influencer marketing in the restaurant industry doesn't require expensive partnerships with celebrities. Local micro-influencers (500 to 25,000 followers) often generate higher engagement and a more relevant audience than national macro-influencers.

### Finding the Right Influencers

**Ottawa food influencers**: search local hashtags like #OttawaEats, #OttawaFood, #EatOttawa and identify accounts with an engaged audience. Check the engagement rate (comments + likes / followers × 100) — a micro-influencer with 5,000 followers and 8% engagement is more valuable than an account with 50,000 followers and 0.5% engagement.

**Toronto**: #TorontoFood, #ToEats, #TOFoodie are good starting hashtags.

**Montreal**: #MTLFood, #MontrealFoodie, #MangerMontreal for French-leaning food accounts.

### Collaboration Models

**Invitation for a meal in exchange for a post**: offer dinner for two in exchange for an Instagram post and Stories. For an average restaurant, this is a €50–€100 investment to potentially reach 5,000–20,000 highly relevant local people.

**Press/influencer event**: organise an opening evening or menu launch for 5–10 local influencers simultaneously. One event generates a burst of content and coverage across multiple audiences.

**Creative collaboration**: some food influencers propose creating professional content in exchange for a meal plus a feature on your profile.

**What to avoid**: asking an influencer to "just share" without offering value in return; writing rigid scripts ("you must say exactly...") — most authentic influencers refuse; ignoring local micro-influencers in favour of large national accounts with no local relevance.

## Seasonal Canadian Content

Canada offers a rich seasonal calendar that restaurants can leverage for authentic and relevant content throughout the year.

### Spring: The Reopening and Patio Season

The patio opening is a genuine cultural event in Canada. "The patio is open!" with a beautiful photo of your outdoor space always generates excellent engagement — especially after the long Canadian winter. Ideal content: patio reopening countdown, tour of the redesigned outdoor space, patio-specific menu reveal.

### Summer: Freshness and Local Sourcing

The Canadian summer is short and intense — capitalise on it. Highlight: local seasonal ingredients (Quebec strawberries, Ontario peaches, Pacific salmon, BC blueberries), cold and refreshing dishes, outdoor brunch experiences, summer events and festivals.

### Fall: Comfort Food and Harvest Colours

Autumn colours create beautiful visual opportunities. Highlight: comforting dishes (soups, stews, fondue), fall ingredients (pumpkin, apples, maple, squash), Canadian Thanksgiving (second Monday of October — a key reservation opportunity), and Halloween-themed menus and specials.

### Winter: Warmth and Festivities

The longest season is also when people seek the most comfort in food. Ideal content: warming dishes and signature hot drinks, holiday decorations, New Year's Eve menu, Valentine's Day specials, and "warming up with us this winter" messaging.

**Key dates for Canadian restaurants**:
- January: New Year's Day, post-holiday lighter options
- February: Valentine's Day (special menus very popular — often fully booked weeks ahead)
- March/April: Easter
- May: Mother's Day (one of the busiest evenings of the year — start promoting 3 weeks ahead)
- June: Father's Day, Quebec's Fête nationale (June 24th for Quebec-based businesses)
- July: Canada Day (July 1st), BBQ and patio season peak
- September/October: Canadian Thanksgiving
- November/December: holiday season

## Paid Social Advertising for Local Restaurants

Organic content builds your community; paid advertising accelerates growth and reaches new customers who don't yet know you exist.

### Facebook/Instagram Ads: Local Targeting

For restaurants, the most effective ad campaigns focus on:

**Precise geographic targeting**: a 5–10 km radius around your establishment. Most restaurants rarely draw customers from more than 15 km away, and targeting too broadly wastes budget on people who will never visit.

**Interest targeting**: "fine dining", "local restaurants", "[cuisine type] food", "brunch", "foodie", etc.

**Lookalike audiences**: if you have a customer email list or the Meta Pixel installed on your website, create audiences "similar" to your existing customers — the most qualified targeting available.

**Formats that convert**:
- 15-second video of a dish being prepared or plated (most engaging)
- Carousel: "Discover our new spring menu" (shows variety)
- Photo with a limited offer: "Book this weekend — complimentary dessert for two"

### Recommended Budget

For an independent restaurant: $250–$500 CAD/month for Meta advertising (Facebook + Instagram) is sufficient to maintain strong local visibility. Concentrate spending around key events (Valentine's Day, Mother's Day, summer patio launch, holiday season) where the conversion intent is highest.

### When to Run Paid Ads

- 2–3 weeks before Valentine's Day (reservation bookings)
- 2 weeks before Mother's Day
- At patio opening
- New menu launches
- When you notice a dip in weekday covers that organic content alone can't fix
